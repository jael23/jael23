About STEM METS

Our History + Mission

For families in Nigeria and the diaspora, and for organisations seeking meaningful educational impact, STEM METS is an education company that puts learning at the centre — combining high-quality, hands-on STEM instruction with access programs that reach children beyond the traditional classroom.

STEM METS was founded in 2013 by two co-founders united by a simple conviction: children learn best when they build, experiment, and create.

For Jadesola Adedeji, the spark was personal — a mother’s search for a better way for her own son to learn, one that led her from a career in pharmacy into education. Alongside co-founder Dr. Funmi Ogunwuyi, she set out to move learning beyond textbooks and rote instruction into hands-on discovery: classrooms where children experiment, collaborate, and think for themselves.

What began as a shared vision has grown into a structured education ecosystem serving families, schools, and corporate partners across Nigeria and the diaspora. Along the way, STEM METS has trained over 40,000 learners, 2,000 teachers and delivered more than 2,000 workshops and camps.

Dr. Funmi Ogunwuyi, board certified nephrologist, passed away shortly after the company was founded, but her vision lives in everything we do. We carry her legacy forward in every child who discovers what they are capable of.

Mission

To transform how children learn — delivering education that builds the creativity, confidence, and problem-solving skills to shape their world.

Vision

To raise generations of leaders and problem-solvers whose learning transforms Nigeria, the continent, and the world.

Our Values

  • Education transforms generations. We measure our work in decades, not terms — because what a child learns outlives the child.
  • Access is not optional. Talent is everywhere; opportunity is not. Geography, gender, and income should never decide who gets to learn.
  • Redemptive over extractive. We choose work that restores and lifts communities — and we walk away from work that doesn’t.
  • Partners who share the mission. Who we work with is part of what we teach.
  • Sustainable, never greedy. Profit is fuel for the mission, not the destination — our profitable programs fund access programs.

CSR projects

Oando Foundation

What a design thinking programme looks like when it runs at state scale.

STEM METS Foundation was implementing and technical partner to the Oando Foundation on the School STEAM Project, across 30 public primary schools in Port Harcourt and Obio-Akpor, Rivers State. We trained over 300 teachers and worked directly with more than 1,500 pupils.

The programme was built on design thinking rather than demonstration. Children identified real problems in their own communities, developed solutions, and presented them. Thirty school projects were narrowed to five finalists, and the pilot closed with a demo day.

Rather than equipping a lab in every school, we built one central innovation lab serving multiple schools. And rather than delivering lessons ourselves, we trained the teachers, so the capability stays in the schools when we leave.

Scaling to a further 30 schools from September 2026.

Project iCommunity

Powered by Chevron and NNPC, Project iCommunity takes design thinking into public schools. In 2023 it reached 10 schools and 500 children in Lagos, with 500 workbooks distributed. The 2024 campaign expanded to Lagos, Ondo and Delta — 15 schools, 750 children, 750 workbooks.

What a partner gets

  • A programme designed around your objectives, not pulled from a catalogue
  • Teacher training that outlasts the funding period
  • Children presenting solutions to problems in their own communities
  • Documented outcomes and reporting throughout

Bricks4Biz® — Corporate training

Experiential leadership development for corporate teams. Teams build, negotiate and rebuild against a real constraint, using LEGO® as the medium — then sit through a facilitated debrief where the behaviour the build surfaced is named and connected back to the workplace.

Used for leadership development, onboarding and integration, and team effectiveness — from executive teams through to graduate intakes.

What your L&D team receives

A written insight report after every session: how the group organised itself, where communication broke down and what it cost, who stepped forward without being asked, and what to reinforce next.

Format

3 to 3.5 hours, extendable to 5 · at your office or an offsite venue · 20 participants standard, 24 the ceiling · one facilitator per ten · delivered nationwide.

The MakerSpace

Where learning gets its joy back.

Children have a home. They have a school. We built them a third place.

The MakerSpace was designed as somewhere children come to build, make and tinker — where imagination is the point, not the distraction, and a child is free to get it wrong, get messy, and go again. They run around. They make friends over a half-built robot. They work on things that are theirs.

Learning stopped being joyful for a lot of children somewhere along the way. This space exists to give that back.

The MakerSpace is our home in Lekki Phase 1 — a bright, creative space built for learning, collaboration and events, set in a green garden surrounded by trees. A quiet escape from Lagos noise, minutes from Freedom Way and Admiralty.

Community Events

The MakerSpace is not only for children.

A child’s first classroom is their home, and the person who sets the weather in it is a parent. So we built part of this space for you. Four times a year we host our STEM METS parents here — a few hours at a weekend, curated as learning and wellness experiences rather than parenting lectures. From our Mothers’ Wellness Retreat to our Fathers’ events: time to learn something, to rest, and to be among people living the same week you’re living.

Parents arrive carrying a lot. They leave lighter, and they leave knowing someone. Because when parents thrive, families thrive — and children grow up in homes that are safe, steady and nurturing.

STEM Africa Fest

The largest STEM event in Africa — and our co-founder helped start it.

In 2021 three women in STEAM ran a virtual festival for 1,300 children. It has since become a pan-African event reaching more than 20,000 children, parents, educators and industry professionals across seven countries. Jadesola Adedeji co-convenes it alongside Titi Adewusi.

How it grew

  • 2021 Founded as STEAM Fun Fest. Virtual, 1,300 children.
  • 2022 First physical event, 6,000 attendees.
  • 2023 Rebranded STEM Africa Fest, expanding to Ghana, Zambia and Rwanda.
  • 2024 Six locations — Lagos, Abuja, Sierra Leone, Gambia and Kenya.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us

What ages do you teach?

Children from 3 to 16. Our early-years courses start at 3 with story-led science and open-ended building, and the work grows with them — by 16 they are doing computer science, game design and 3D modelling.

Where are you?

The MakerSpace, 5 James Adejumo Street, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos — minutes from Freedom Way and Admiralty. Most of our clubs, camps and parties run there. School programmes run in your school.

Does my child need any experience?

No. Most children arrive having never written a line of code or built a robot. Courses are grouped by age and start from first principles, so a child with no background is never behind. This applies to the child who says they are “not techy” too. Children who dislike screens take to the building, electronics and design tracks, where the work is physical. Children who think they are bad at maths often turn out to be good at problem-solving — which is the same thing, approached from the other end.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the programme, how long it runs and how often it meets. Tell us your child’s age and what you are looking for, and we will send you the current fees.

Who teaches the classes?

Graduates, every one of them, trained by us in STEM learning and teaching before they lead a session. All of our facilitators complete background and security checks, safeguarding training, and certificated first aid training.

Can we visit before we commit?

Yes. We offer trials and taster sessions, so your child can try it and you can see the space before you commit to a term. Get in touch and we will tell you what is coming up.
